a1.1 [assistant chat 2026-09-08T12:30] user: Quick one: when should I use Welch's t-test instead of a regular Student's t-test?
a1.2 [assistant chat 2026-09-08T12:30] assistant: Use Welch's whenever the two groups might have different variances or different sample sizes, which in practice is most of the time. Student's t-test assumes equal variances, and when that's wrong it can give misleading p-values, especially if the smaller group has the larger variance. Welch's adjusts the degrees of freedom to account for this and loses very little power when the variances turn out to be equal, so many statisticians recommend it as the default. In Python it's scipy.stats.ttest_ind(a, b, equal_var=False), and in R, t.test() already runs Welch's unless you set var.equal = TRUE.
